# An unrestricted third prime power at even cofactors through 32,768

Let $D\le32768$ be positive and even, with at most two distinct prime divisors. Write $Q=q^e$ with $q$ prime and $e\ge1$. Consider the full three-row conjecture at $N=1+DQ$.

* **All prime powers work** except possibly at $D=15424,24352,27904$. In particular 4,369 of the 4,372 choices $D=2^jP$ with $P>1$ an odd prime power are settled at every base and exponent.
* **All these cofactors work simultaneously** if $Q\le10^{10000}$, if $e$ is a multiple of any of $2,5,171$, or if $q$ belongs to an explicit set of primes of relative natural density $1201553/1231200>97.59\%$. The last two conclusions have no size bound on $Q$.
* **For $q\le10000$, every exponent works** except possibly at $D=27904$ with $q=379,2539,2579,7507$. At these four bases every $e\le10^{100}$ works, as does every square or cube exponent without a size bound. For every prime $q$ whatsoever, at most $O(H^{0.1129})$ of any $H$ consecutive exponents can fail at even one cofactor in the range.
* At each of the three remaining cofactors a single forced row remains. The [return-word certificates](#why-separate-modular-tests-leave-a-class-at-every-modulus) prove that every modulus has a reduced class meeting each of its three required carry-free sets. Each set here consists of **positive integers whose entire addition is carry-free** in its own base. The three witnesses within a class can differ.

## Reducing the cofactors

The [two-factor theorem](all_weights_endpoint_reduction.md) covers $D$ a power of two or $q\mid D$. Otherwise write $D=wP$ with $w=2^j$ and $P$ a power of the odd prime $p$. The [three-factor assignment](endpoint_factors.md) forces a row with parts
$$
       aPQ,\quad bwQ,\quad cQ+1,\qquad c=(w-a)P-bw>0 ,
$$
with positive multipliers $a,b$. The row avoids $2,p,q$ and the primes in a unitary divisor $U\ge3$ of $N$ dividing $a,b$. As $N$ is odd, take an odd prime $u\mid U$. The three conditions
$$
                aPQ\equiv1\pmod w,\quad bwQ\equiv1\pmod P,\quad
                DQ\equiv-1\pmod u
$$
give one class $r\bmod uD$. The position bounds are $0<a<w$, $0<b<(w-a)P/w$. As in the [earlier unbounded-partner proof](even_two_power_cofactors.md), the case $a=u=w-1$ is excluded by a carry at $u$.

The earlier theorem covers $w\le4$, $D\le15423$, and $w=8,16$ through $D=40000$. The additional interval contains 201 pairs with $w\ge32$. We enumerated their possible assignments and applied the same digit, congruence and finite carry-graph tests. The second enumeration factors $\gcd(a,b)$ after generating the positive multipliers and compares every list for every pair.

For each row we fix the low $w$ or $u$ block in the two radices $2,u$. The [gcd of the differences of accepted suffixes](denominator_residue_hulls.md) gives the refined class by CRT. To verify the size bound, read the two complete digit expansions backward from their accepting states, intersecting their possible intervals of integers. We prepend the prescribed low digits to each graph and use the weaker class $r\bmod uD$. Through $10^{10000}$ the three searches visit respectively 402,607, 162,418 and 477,130 intervals. All are excluded before any direct integer test is needed. An independent cover uses intervals on which a factorial-floor inequality forces a carry at $2,u$ or $P$. It covers the same three ranges in 178,063, 75,676 and 87,096 intervals.

## Which prime bases and exponents can reach the classes?

In each refined modulus the odd factors are powers of $u$ and $P$; the power of two is denoted $2^k$. Split its unit group into the sign and the cyclic subgroup generated by 5. The sign of every target is $-1$. The table lists orders of the target in the other three cyclic factors, with the orders of those groups in parentheses.

| $D$ | Binary cyclic factor | At $u$ | At $P$ | Divisors excluded in $e$ | Prime fraction left by the class |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 15424 | $128\ (128)$ | $486\ (486)$ | $10\ (240)$ | $2,3,5$ | $1/720$ |
| 24352 | $33554432\ (67108864)$ | $20\ (20)$ | $95\ (760)$ | $2,5,19$ | $9/15200$ |
| 27904 | $33554432\ (33554432)$ | $5\ (10)$ | $36\ (108)$ | $2,5,9$ | $1/45$ |

Here is the group count. In the $\ell$-primary component, let $\ell^{n_i}$ be the cyclic group orders and $\ell^{b_i}$ the orders of the target coordinates. When $b=\max b_i>0$, put $h=\min_{b_i>0}(n_i-b_i)$. A target is an $e$th power exactly when $v_\ell(e)\le h$. To count all base elements some power of which equals the target, group them by order $\ell^{b+j}$. Raising such an element to $\ell^j$ must give one of the $\varphi(\ell^b)$ generators of the target subgroup. Each generator has exactly $\ell^{\sum_i\min(j,n_i)}$ preimages for $0\le j\le h$, all of the stated order. Divide the sum of these counts by the group size. A trivial target component allows every base element. The fractions multiply over $\ell$, as the required exponent congruences combine by CRT. The excluded-degree column gives $2,5,171$ as sufficient divisors simultaneously across the three rows.

For the union of prime classes the two binary targets at 15424 and 27904 require $q\equiv3\bmod8$. The target at 24352 requires $q\equiv7\bmod16$. Each indicated condition suffices in the binary group alone. At 15424 and 27904 the cyclic coordinate generates the full cyclic factor. At 24352 it generates its index-two subgroup. In each case the exponent is odd. Conditional on a fixed allowed binary residue of $q$, the odd-coordinate fraction is constant. Indeed that binary residue fixes the odd exponent modulo the order of its binary cyclic coordinate, a multiple of the entire exponent of the 2-primary part of the odd-coordinate groups. Powering by the prescribed odd exponent is a bijection on those 2-primary groups. All odd-primary exponents are independent by CRT. The odd moduli at 15424 and 27904 are relatively prime, so their conditions are independent after fixing the binary residue. Thus the overlap has fraction $4(1/720)(1/45)$. Subtracting the union from one gives $1201553/1231200$. Equidistribution of primes in the reduced classes of a fixed modulus gives relative natural density. [Prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions](https://kskedlaya.org/ant/chapter-5.html)

## Individual prime bases and intervals of exponents

At the prime $q$, the addition of the three slopes themselves must be carry-free, since multiplication by $q^e$ shifts the digits and the extra one sits below that shift. Test this first. For each remaining $q\le10000$ solve $q^e\equiv R$ in each of the three prime-power factors of the refined modulus, where $R$ is the target residue. Baby-step giant-step exhausts one period in each factor and generalized CRT combines the logarithms. Exactly five pairs remain, all with $D=27904$.

| $q$ | Residue of $e$ | Period |
|---:|---:|---:|

| 2539 | 1366259897 | 1509949440 |
| 2579 | 2879938563 | 4529848320 |
| 7043 | 2079006999 | 4529848320 |

At 7043 the period gives $7043^{4529848320}\equiv1\bmod81$, so the companion scale $V=(1+27904Q)/11$ is always $39\bmod81$. The prime 3 divides $V$ and is assigned to the companion. For every unordered positive triple $(f,g,h)$ of sum 11, the residues of $39(f,g,h)$ sum to at least the modulus at one of $9,27,81$. This excludes the entire exponent class.

At 379, 2579 and 7507 the period is divisible by 9 and the exponent is divisible by 3 exactly once. It cannot be a proper perfect power. At 2539 the exponent is 2 modulo both 5 and 9, excluding squares modulo 5 and cubes modulo 9. In particular 2539 is the only possible base in this range at any proper perfect-power exponent, and then its degree must be coprime to 6.

For the other four classes let $L$ be the period in the table. Direct modular exponentiation gives $q^L\equiv1+2^{27}\bmod 2^{28}$. Each successive squaring increases the valuation of $q^L-1$ by one for the corresponding doubled exponent. Thus extending the exponent class by one bit toggles exactly the next undetermined bit of $Q$. Retain only digits that extend in the binary graph to some complete accepted input. When the stride exceeds $10^{100}$, the remaining branches yield respectively 8,935, 13,436, 8,854 and 8,880 positive exponents in the range. At each such exponent a carry at a layer through $11^{13}$ excludes the row, by computing $q^e$ modulo that power.

There is a uniform bound beyond the calculation. The three productive binary graphs with the initial digits prefixed have 44, 50 and 110 states. Starting with count 1 at every state, update each count to the sum of the counts at its successors. After 10,000 updates the maximum counts are strictly below $2^{1129},2^{849},2^{424}$, respectively. Splitting inputs into blocks of 10,000 bits therefore bounds the number of extendible $n$-bit words by $O(2^{n\sigma})$ with $\sigma=0.1129,0.0849,0.0424$ in table order. For all permitted $q$, its order modulo $2^n$ is at least $2^{n-3}$ for $n\ge4$, by the binary residue conditions and successive squaring. In every odd-step progression of $H$ positive exponents choose $n=\lceil\log_2 H\rceil+3$ (increasing it to 4 if needed). The values of $q^e$ are distinct modulo $2^n$, proving the stated uniform bound. For a fixed exponent, the same $O(H^{0.1129})$ bounds exceptional bases in any odd-step progression of $H$ integers. Only odd exponents need testing and odd powering permutes the binary units.

## Why separate modular tests leave a class at every modulus

For a row in the table and each $s=2,u,P$, let $L_s$ be the set of positive integers $T$ for which its parts with parameter $T$ add without a carry in base $s$. For **every** modulus $m$ there is a reduced residue class meeting every $L_s$ in arbitrarily large integers and containing infinitely many ordinary primes. We can impose the displayed CRT condition and $u\parallel 1+DT$ on the whole class after refining the modulus. More generally, fix $e$ coprime to $uP$ and divisible by none of the excluded divisors for that row. The same class construction can be made to contain infinitely many $e$th powers of primes. This statement concerns the intersection of the three sets of admissible residues. A counterexample would require a single prime power in the three integer sets and would also have to pass the companion-row conditions.

To prove the assertion, read $T$ from its units digit in base $s$. A carry state records the quotients in the three scalar multiplications after the digits already read. At a fixed state a word of length $l$ and value $v$ returning to that same state acts on a subsequent tail $x$ by $x\mapsto v+s^l x$. At any modulus $m'$ prime to $s$ this is a permutation. The semigroup of such loops modulo $m'$ contains their inverses by finiteness. Two loops of the same length with values $v,v'$ therefore generate translation by $v-v'$. If the gcd of the differences across certified pairs of loops is a power of $s$, the translations generate every residue modulo $m'$. Appending the loops before a fixed accepting tail can give any desired class modulo $m'$. Inserting sufficiently many repeats of a nonzero loop acting as the identity after a full permutation period gives arbitrarily large integers in each class.

The [integer certificates](check_cofactor_return_words.py) give a fixed low word, an accepting tail and pairs of returning words for all nine choices of $(D,s)$. They verify the states and the absence of carries by factorial floors. For example at $(15424,241)$ the low digit is 150, the accepting tail is 172, and there are three-digit loops of values $8712322,8712386,8712467$. The two differences $64,145$ are coprime. At $(15424,2)$ one pair of loops has length 23 and difference 152, and another length 43 and difference 106266624. Their gcd is a power of two.

Repeat one loop in each radix to fix an arbitrarily long low word while returning to its starting state. At that radix's power in the modulus prescribe the resulting unit residue. At other primes outside the three bases prescribe 1. Combine by CRT. In each radix individually the free loops now supply every requirement at the complementary modulus, proving the assertion about the three $L_s$. The certified initial words give the required CRT constraints and exact $u$-valuation. For the power version, $e$ is odd, so every binary unit has an $e$th root. At each odd radix the initial digit has such a root by the order table; the root lifts through all powers since $s\nmid e$. At the remaining primes the residue 1 has a root. Applying Dirichlet's theorem to the resulting reduced root class supplies infinitely many prime powers of the requested exponent. The repeated initial words can additionally enforce any prescribed finite carry depth at the three bases on those actual prime powers.

## Reproduction

Run the three standard-library checks from the repository root.

~~~sh
python3 check_even_cofactor_partners.py
python3 check_cofactor_return_words.py
python3 check_even_partner_exponents.py
~~~

Run `python3 check_even_cofactor_partners_cross.py` with the project's python-flint environment for the independent floor cover and direct enumeration of the odd-coordinate groups for each possible odd exponent phase.
