# Cubic spacing of exceptional outer exponents

Fix distinct odd primes $B,c$ such that $B\nmid c-1,c+1$ and $c\nmid B-1,B+1$ (neither neighbor is divisible in either direction). Put $E=Bc$. For any prime $p>E$ and $h\ge1$, consider
$$N_a=1+p^a(Bp^h+c),\qquad a\ge1.$$

**Theorem.** Every interval of $K$ consecutive outer exponents contains at most
$$O_{B,c}\!\left(1+\frac{K}{h^3(\log p)^2}\right)$$
possible exceptions to the full trinomial conjecture. The starting exponent, prime base and both exponents are unrestricted. Every individual weight satisfies the conjecture whenever
$$ (2E)^{64(a+2h)}\le p^{h^2}. \tag{1}$$
The individual assertion only needs $B,c$ to be distinct odd primes. No factorization of $Bp^h+c$ is required.

For example, with $B=11,c=17$, **every prime $p\ge191$, every $h\ge600$ and every $1\le a\le h^2/100$** give the full conjecture. Indeed $374^6<191^7$ and $7\cdot64(a+2h)\le6h^2$ in that range. At $p=191$, $h=10{,}000$, (1) covers the first 1,300,000 outer exponents. The spacing result improves the linear dependence on the inner exponent from the earlier two-block estimate for these coefficients. [Two-block spacing](two_block_spacing.md)

## A large divisor seen at two exponents

Write $P=p^h$, $D=BP+c$. Use the normalized split and inequalities (1) of the [two-block ratio theorem](distinct_block_ratios.md#three-determinants). Each of the two unshifted forms is $iP+j$ with $(i,j)$ a nonnegative proper part of $(B,c)$. No such nonzero form is proportional to $D$ because $\gcd(B,c)=1$. Thus $S\le E^2$ and the scale $U$ is at least $3D^{2/3}/E^{4/3}$. Once $27D^2>E^7$, we have $U>E$. The determinant between the two unshifted forms must vanish, and neither can use just one block. They are positive integer multiples
$$x=m(bP+d),\qquad y=n(bP+d)$$
of a primitive form, with $b,d\ge1$, $\gcd(b,d)=1$, $2b\le B$, $2d\le c$. Set $A=bc-Bd\ne0$ and $g=\gcd(m,n)$. Then
$$F_a=U/\gcd(U,g)\mid bP+d,\qquad F_a\mid N_a,\qquad
 F_a\ge\frac{3P^{2/3}}{E^{4/3}\min(B,c)}. \tag{2}$$
Here $g\le\min(B,c)$ follows from the split of each coefficient. All of $p,b,d,A$ are units modulo $F_a$, using $\gcd(b,d)=1$ and the following two congruences:
$$b p^h\equiv-d,\qquad A p^a\equiv-b\pmod{F_a}.\tag{3}$$
The second follows by multiplying $p^aD\equiv-1$ by $b$ and substituting the first. Two exceptions at $a<a'$ with the same primitive $(b,d)$ give a common divisor
$$G=\gcd(F_a,F_{a'})\ \ge\ F_a F_{a'}/(bP+d)\ \gg_{B,c}P^{1/3}. \tag{4}$$
The multiplicative order of $p$ modulo $G$ divides $a'-a$.

## Counting distinct elements of the multiplicative subgroup

For a nonzero rational $z$ in lowest terms put $H(z)=\max(|\mathrm{numerator}(z)|,\mathrm{denominator}(z))$. Suppose $z_1,\dots,z_k$ are multiplicatively independent up to sign, each numerator and denominator is coprime to $M$, and all lie in the subgroup generated by $p$ modulo $M$. Count products $\prod z_i^{e_i}$ with $0\le e_i\le\log(M/2)/(4k\log(2H(z_i)))$. Each numerator and denominator is at most $(M/2)^{1/4}$. A nonzero difference of two cross products has absolute value at most $2(M/2)^{1/2}<M$ for $M>2$. Hence distinct rational products remain distinct modulo $M$. For fixed $k$ this gives
$$\operatorname{ord}_M(p)\gg_k
 \frac{(\log M)^k}{\prod_{i=1}^k\log(2H(z_i))}\qquad(M\ge3). \tag{5}$$
This proof also covers a coordinate whose exponent range contains only zero, since $1+\lfloor t\rfloor\ge t$.

First consider $b=d=1$. Then $F_a\mid P+1$, so $\operatorname{ord}_{F_a}(p)\le2h$. But $p,-1/(c-B)$ both lie in the subgroup. Their absolute values are multiplicatively independent because $1<|c-B|<p$. Equations (2) and (5) give $\operatorname{ord}_{F_a}(p)\gg_{B,c}h^2\log p$. This rules out the shape for all sufficiently large $P$, uniformly in $p$.

For each other shape, the three subgroup elements at $G$ are
$$p,\qquad -d/b,\qquad -b/A.$$
They are multiplicatively independent up to sign. In fact $b,d,|A|$ are pairwise coprime by primality of $B,c$ and $b<B,d<c$. If $|A|>1$, a prime in $A$ occurs only in the last rational, and $d/b$ has absolute value different from one. If $|A|=1$, both $b$ and $d$ must exceed one, since $b=1$ would give $c\equiv\pm1\bmod B$ and $d=1$ would give $B\equiv\pm1\bmod c$. A prime of $d$ then separates $d/b$ from $b$, and $b>1$. Finally $p>E$ divides none of $b,d,A$. Formula (5) and (4) imply
$$a'-a\ \gg_{B,c}\ h^3(\log p)^2.$$
Sum the interval bound for this spacing over the finitely many primitive pairs $(b,d)$. The bounded range of $P$ omitted in proving (2) and (4) can be absorbed in the implied constant since it comprises finitely many $p,h$.

## The explicit initial interval

Under (1), $P>(2E)^{128}$ since $a>0$. This gives $27D^2>E^7$ and $F_a>P^{5/8}$ by (2), as $P^{1/24}>E^{4/3}\min(B,c)$. Assume an exceptional exponent. Choose a nearest nonnegative integer $q$ to $a/h$ and write $r=a-qh$, $r_+=\max(r,0)$, $r_-=\max(-r,0)$. The two congruences (3) give
$$ F_a\mid p^{r_+}(-d)^q A+p^{r_-}b^{q+1}. \tag{6}$$
The absolute value is at most $2E^{q+1}P^{1/2}\le(2E)^{a/h+2}P^{1/2}\le P^{1/64+1/2}<F_a$. Thus (6) is an integer equality to zero. Taking its $p$-adic valuation on the two terms gives $r=0$ because $p>E$. Now $q=a/h\ge1$. We have $d^q|A|=b^{q+1}$, forcing $d=1$ by $\gcd(b,d)=1$. Also $b\mid A=bc-B$, so $b\mid B$ and $b<B$ imply $b=1$. This would give $|c-B|=1$, impossible for two distinct odd primes.

The verifier tests the congruences and the numerator in (6) against direct gcds of integer weights at 18,832 inputs. It also checks 8,999 pairs with a shared modulus greater than two. It checks independence by exact prime valuations and the numerical example by integers. Run `python3 check_block_exponent_orders.py`.
