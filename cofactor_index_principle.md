# A finite divisibility principle for bounded-digit tests at every cofactor bound

Fix $D\ge1$. For $N=1+dq^e$, where $1\le d\le D$ and $q>d+1$ is prime, consider the necessary endpoint row conditions below. **There is a finite list of divisors of the exponent $e$ giving an exact dichotomy for these conditions.** Every prime factor of a listed divisor is at most $D+1$.

1. If some listed integer divides $e$, a finite number of low digits at primes through $D+1$ rules out every candidate for every $d\le D$. In particular Wasserman's full conjecture holds at all the indicated weights with $q>d+1$.
2. Otherwise there is a fixed $d\le D$ and a fixed choice of the two row shapes for which arbitrarily long simultaneous low-digit tests admit solutions. For any prescribed prime cutoff and digit depth there is a reduced progression of prime bases $q$ giving actual positive rows that pass these tests. The progression can be chosen so that the **full conjecture holds at every $1+d'q^e$ with $d'$ in any prescribed finite range**.

Quantitatively, in the second case infinitely many distinct primes $q$ work simultaneously for **every $d'\le q^{1/120}$**, while the three selected rows at the fixed $d$ pass the first $\lfloor c_D\log q\rfloor$ digits at every prime through $\lfloor c_D\log q\rfloor$, with $c_D>0$. Passing the test means that at least one of the three rows has no carry in the inspected digits of that base. The rows may differ across bases.

If the full conjecture holds through $D+1$, the first alternative occurs for some exponents. The finite-list assertion is structural and does not give a bound for its largest member. The [explicit cofactor theorems](cofactor_power_indices.md) also use completion of digit words, and earlier global exclusions of row shapes. Their sufficient classes and their constructed hard examples are proved directly.

## The finite list of row shapes and primes

Suppose the three coefficients have gcd one. By the [all-weight endpoint reduction](all_weights_endpoint_reduction.md), one row is $E=(1,1,N-2)$. Assign each prime of $N(N-1)$ to a row among the other two that avoids it, calling the row assigned $q$ the first. Put $P=q^e$. Kummer's criterion at $q$ and at the weight primes gives
$$
 A=(aP,bP,cP+1),\qquad C=V(f_1,f_2,f_3),\qquad UV=1+dP .
$$
Here $a+b+c=d$, $U$ is a unitary divisor of the weight, $U\mid a,b$, $\gcd(U,d)=1$, and the positive $f_i$ sum to $U$. All three slopes are positive. In particular, $c=0$ would give the part 1 in a row whose parts are multiples of $U\ge3$. Thus the tuples $(d,U,a,b,c,f_1,f_2,f_3)$ under consideration form a finite set. For $d\le2$ it is empty. The assigned weight factors are $U$ for $A$ and $V$ for $C$.

For a given tuple and each prime $r\le d+1$, independently seek an $r$-adic unit $P_r$ that is an $e$th power of an $r$-adic unit. Interpret the two affine rows at $P_r$ and impose these conditions at depth $k\ge1$.

- If $r\mid U$, require $v_r(1+dP_r)=v_r(U)$ and the first $k$ digits of $A$ to add without carries.
- If $r\nmid U$ and $r\mid1+dP_r$, require those digits of $C$ to add without carries.
- If $r\mid d$, require at least one of $A,C$ to add without carries through that depth.

The second-row denominator $U$ is a unit wherever that row is tested. All these conditions depend only on a finite prefix, of at most $k+v_r(U)+1$ digits. Every actual counterexample at $q>d+1$ supplies all these witnesses at all depths by taking $P_r=q^e$. At the new prime $q$, the displayed first row adds carry-free automatically for $q>d+1$.

Let $\mathcal H_D$ be the exponent indices for which every tuple fails at some finite depth. There is one depth sufficient for all the tuples at each such index, by finiteness. This proves the common-divisor implication in the first alternative.

## Why finitely many divisors suffice

For odd $r$, membership in the unit $e$th powers at any fixed precision $r^j$ depends only on $\gcd(e,(r-1)r^{j-1})$, by cyclicity of the group. At 2 it depends only on $v_2(e)$, by the sign factor and the subgroup generated by 5. Therefore the entire collection of local tests, over all depths and tuples, depends only on the valuations $(v_s(e))_{s\le D+1}$ indexed by primes. Increasing any of these coordinates can only shrink the power images. Thus $\mathcal H_D$ corresponds to an upward-closed subset of a fixed finite-dimensional lattice of nonnegative integer vectors.

Such a subset has finitely many minimal elements. Indeed every infinite sequence of nonnegative integers has an infinite nondecreasing subsequence: take a constant subsequence if some value repeats infinitely often, and otherwise successively choose larger later terms. Apply this to each of the finitely many coordinates. An infinite sequence of distinct minimal elements would then contain two comparable elements. Every vector of the upward-closed set dominates a minimal vector by descent. Converting these vectors to products of prime powers gives the claimed finite list.

To see when the list is nonempty, suppose the conjecture holds at every reference weight $d+1\ge3$ through $D+1$. Choose a common precision $h$ large enough that at each prime $r\le D+1$, the residue $P_r\equiv1\pmod{r^h}$ preserves all digits of the reference rows up to their largest occupied position, including the division by $U$ wherever needed. Such an $h$ exists since the reference weights and all tuples are bounded. It also determines whether $U$ divides $d+1$ with the required unitarity. Let $e$ be a common multiple of the unit group exponents modulo all those $r^h$. Every local unit power now has that residue. If the scale conditions hold, $A(1)$ and $C(1)$ are actual positive rows of weight $d+1$. A prime dividing their coefficients and the endpoint coefficient divides $d(d+1)$ and is at most $d+1$. The reference carry in each row occurs at finite precision and violates the corresponding test. This puts $e$ in $\mathcal H_D$.

## Realizing the other indices at actual weights

For $e\notin\mathcal H_D$ choose a tuple passing at every finite depth. Fix cutoffs $B,k$. For each prime $r\le d+1$ choose one root of its local power target to the required precision and prescribe that residue for $q$. At each further prime $r\le B$ use $q\equiv1\pmod r$. Such a prime divides neither $d$ nor $d+1$, so it misses the endpoint coefficient at every sufficiently large prime base of the resulting class. The core congruences ensure that $U$ divides $N$ unitarily and that the actual row $C$ is integral. The Chinese remainder theorem gives one reduced progression for $q$, of positive relative density among primes. The actual rows in it satisfy all the stated tests, using $E$ whenever the prime misses the endpoint. [Prime number theorem in progressions](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2009/09/24/the-prime-number-theorem-in-arithmetic-progressions-and-dueling-conspiracies/)

To force the full conjecture on the same progression, select the binary prefix at a precision $t\ge2+v_2(e)$ large enough to preserve the tests and to apply the [previous binary extension theorem](cofactor_binary_prefix_extensions.md) to the desired cofactor bound $D'$. Every extension of this unit prefix in the scalar $P$ is still an $e$th power locally. For odd $e$ the map on binary units is surjective; for even $e$ its image is the units congruent to 1 modulo $2^{2+v_2(e)}$. The theorem gives a scalar extension that certifies every cofactor through $D'$ at prime-power inputs. Lift the extended word to a root residue for $q$ and keep all the odd-prime prescriptions. This proves the second alternative.

For the growing assertion, take both cutoffs equal to $H\to\infty$ and let $A_D=2+\sum_{r\le D+1}\log r$ over primes. Put $t=\lceil2A_D H\rceil$ and $D'=2^t$. The odd-prime powers needed in the core have precision at most $H+O_D(1)$, and the other primes through $H$ each need only one digit. The binary theorem uses at most $21t+8$ digits including the original $t$. Its final binary modulus also suffices for the root residue. Using the prime number theorem in the form $\sum_{r\le H}\log r<2H$ for large $H$, the root progression modulus $m$ satisfies
$$\log m\le A_D H+(21t+8)\log2+O_D(1)<22\log D'+O_D(1).$$
The least prime in the class exceeds $H$ and satisfies $q\ll m^{5.2}$. Hence $D'\ge q^{1/120}$ for large $H$, and $\log q\ll_D H$. The primes obtained as $H$ grows are unbounded. [Xylouris, *On Linnik's constant*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.2749)
