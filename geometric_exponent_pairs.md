# Two exponent intervals with every geometric multiple included

Let $p,q$ be distinct primes and $D$ a prime power coprime to $pq$. Write $m_{p,q}$ for the multiplicative order of $q^2$ modulo $p$, with $m_{2,q}=1$. Let $I,J$ be intervals of $L,H\ge2$ consecutive positive integers respectively. Apart from at most
$$
 O_{p,q}\!\left(D L^{\beta_q}H^{\beta_p}
          \log(2LD)\log(2HD)\right) \tag{1}
$$
pairs $(f,e)\in I\times J$, the conjecture holds **at every weight**
$$
                1+D p^{m_{q,p} f q^h} q^{m_{p,q} e p^k},\qquad h,k\ge0 .
$$
Here $\beta_p=\log_p((2\sin(\pi/(4p+2)))^{-1})<1$. The same result holds for the [additional four-prime cofactor types](three_factor_exponent_rectangles.md#extension-to-four-predecessor-primes) with $D$ coprime to $pq$. Both interval starting points are arbitrary. For one variable, aside from $O_p(D H^{\beta_p}\log(2HD))$ values $e\in J$, all $k\ge0$ and **all** exponents $b\ge1$ work at $1+Dp^b q^{m_{p,q}e p^k}$, with the constant independent of $q$.

For example, let $p=2$, $q=3$ and $L=H$. Both orders are one, so the weights are $1+D2^{f3^h}3^{e2^k}$. A proportion tending to one of all the pairs $(f,e)$ in these intervals work simultaneously for every eligible $D\le H^{0.28}$ and every $h,k\ge0$. Summing over $D$ costs $O(H^{0.56})$, while $\beta_2+\beta_3<1.432$. This extends the [two finite intervals theorem](three_factor_exponent_rectangles.md) to infinite sets of exponent pairs generated by the same starting pair.

## Counting starting exponents

Fix a cut $0<x<D$. Write $w_p(x)=(p/\lambda_p)^{v_p(x(D-x))}$, $\lambda_p=p^{\beta_p}$. By the [block bound and simultaneous cut condition](three_factor_exponent_rectangles.md#how-many-exponent-terms-can-pass-a-cut), at a failure the cut must add freely at both primes. At $p$ its parts are $xt,(D-x)t$ where $t=q^{m_{p,q}e p^k}$, and at $q$ the roles reverse with the **same** $x$. We bound the number of $e$ for which even one $k$ can pass the first test.

At odd $p$, putting $c=q^{m_{p,q}}$ gives $c\equiv1$ or $-1\bmod p$. If $c\equiv1$, take $a=c$. If $c\equiv-1$ and $e$ is even, take $a=c^2$ and use the consecutive starting exponents $e/2$. If $c\equiv-1$ and $e$ is odd, $c^{e p^k}\equiv-1\bmod p^{k+1}$ by binomial expansion of $(-c)^{e p^k}$. As soon as $p^{k+1}\ge D$ the residues of the two parts of the cut add to $2p^{k+1}-D\ge p^{k+1}$. Each of the $O_p(\log(2D))$ earlier $k$ costs $O_p(w_p(x)H^{\beta_p})$ by the [single-exponent estimate for arbitrary progressions](three_factor_exponent_rectangles.md#how-many-exponent-terms-can-pass-a-cut), applied to the odd $e$ in the interval.

At $p=2$ take $a=q^2$ and reindex $k\ge1$. The missing $k=0$ with $t=q^e$ costs $O_p(w_p(x)H^{\beta_p})$ by the same single-exponent estimate. In the remaining cases write $s=v_p(a-1)$, so $s\ge1$, and in binary $s\ge3$. Denote the consecutive exponents for $a$ by $e$ again.

For $k\ge0$ put $L_k(e)=(a^{e p^k}-1)/p^{s+k}$. These are integers, and two properties hold for all $e$.

1. For distinct positive $e,e'$ one has $v_p(L_k(e)-L_k(e'))=v_p(e-e')$. Factor out the smaller power of $a$, then apply $v_p(a^t-1)=s+v_p(t)$. The latter follows by the geometric sum at an exponent coprime to $p$ and binomial expansion at exponent $p$.
2. $L_{k+1}(e)\equiv L_k(e)\bmod p^{s+k-1}$. Raise $1+p^{s+k}L_k(e)$ to its $p$th power and divide the nonlinear terms by $p^{s+k+1}$. Each resulting term contains $p^{s+k-1}$.

Let $n=\lceil\log_p H\rceil$. For fixed $k$, the first $s+k$ digits of $a^{e p^k}$ are fixed, and its next $n$ digits are the residue of $L_k(e)$. Property 1 makes these residues distinct across the interval of at most $H$ exponents. The block estimate bounds the choices by $w_p(x)\lambda_p^n$.

Put $K=\max(n,\lceil\log_p D\rceil)$. At every $k\ge K$ the low pieces $x,D-x$ have sum less than $p^{s+k}$. The first $n$ positions above that power are the low positions of $xL_k(e),(D-x)L_k(e)$. By property 2 they are identical for all $k\ge K$. If any such cut works, the $n$ positions work already at $K$. Counting these residues by property 1 and the block bound gives $w_p(x)\lambda_p^n$ for the entire tail. Summing the earlier tests gives $O_p(w_p(x)H^{\beta_p}\log(2HD))$ for this cut, uniformly in $q$.

The possible pairs at each cut lie in the Cartesian product of the two resulting sets of starting exponents. By Cauchy–Schwarz and the valuation-level count in the two-interval theorem, $\sum_x w_p(x)w_q(x)\ll_{p,q} D$. This proves (1). Summing just $w_p(x)$ gives the one-variable statement.

## Explicit exponent classes for a fixed prime

For every prime $q\equiv2^{61}-1\bmod2^{121}$, including $2^{61}-1$ itself, **more than 99.985%** of the odd residues $e\bmod 2^{60}$ certify the conjecture simultaneously at the weights below.
$$
  1+D\,2^b q^{e'2^k}
  \text{ satisfies the conjecture for all } b\ge1,\ k\ge0,
  \ e'>0\text{ in the class of }e,\text{ and odd prime powers }D\le250.
$$
For $D\le100$ the guaranteed proportion exceeds 99.9966%. These fractions refer to residue classes, and all positive representatives work.

At $k=0$ every class works by the [negative residue test](prime_bases_at_geometric_exponents.md#an-explicit-prime-progression-for-all-outer-exponents). For $k\ge1$ its binomial expansion gives
$$
       q^{e'2^k}=1+2^{61+k}Z,\qquad Z\equiv-e\bmod 2^{60}.
$$
Failure would require the bottom 60 bits of $xZ,(D-x)Z$ to be disjoint for a cut $0<x<D$. The [one-numerator reduction](static_square_cofactors.md#at-most-two-possible-numerators) requires $x$ coprime to $D$ and divisible by an odd divisor of $N$ of size at least three. Hence we can use unordered cuts, omitting a pair if both sides are powers of two. Negation permutes the $2^{59}$ odd residues, so it suffices to count odd scales with disjoint product bits for each remaining cut.

| Maximum $D$ | Number of cuts | Sum of the numbers of surviving odd scales |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 31 | 92 | 4,127,843,005,161 |
| 100 | 583 | 19,462,275,734,923 |
| 250 | 3,218 | 85,421,792,224,053 |

The last column bounds the union even if different cuts count the same scale. Divide it by $2^{59}$ to obtain an upper bound for the exceptional fraction.

The first computation reads one multiplier bit at a time, maintains the two multiplication carries, and sums the counts of all legal 60-bit words with low bit one. The second computation uses an equation for the output bits. Write $a,b$ for the even and odd slopes of a cut. The residues $(u,w)=(az,bz)\bmod2^{60}$ are characterized by $bu-aw\equiv0\bmod2^{60}$ with $w$ odd, since multiplication by $b$ is invertible. Read bit pairs $(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)$ only, and maintain the signed quotient of $bu-aw$ formed from the lower bits by their modulus. This counts exactly those pairs with disjoint bits. The two counts agree separately at each cut.
