# Wasserman’s common-divisor conjecture for trinomial coefficients

**Work in progress.** This repository studies the open general case and records partial proofs and reproducible computations.

For an integer $N\ge3$, take any three triples of positive integers summing to $N$. Wasserman conjectured that a prime divides all three numbers $N!/(a!b!c!)$ obtained from the triples.

Every counterexample must contain $(1,1,N-2)$ up to permutation. This reduces the full conjecture at every weight to proving that any two coefficients share a prime factor of $N(N-1)$. In particular the conjecture holds whenever $N-1$ has at most two distinct prime divisors.

The conjecture holds at a set of weights of natural density one. The number of possible exceptions up to $X$ is $O(X^{0.8689})$. The proportion of exceptions also tends to zero uniformly in every interval of length at least $X^{29/40}$ within $[X,2X]$.

At weights congruent to zero or one modulo three, the number of possible exceptions up to $X$ is $O(X^{0.8178})$. [Counts by prime residue class](prime_residue_counts.md)

When $30$ divides $N(N-1)$, the exceptional count is $O(X^{0.7593})$. More generally, for every $\epsilon>0$ there is a fixed primorial $Q$ for which the count at $Q\mid N(N-1)$ is $O_\epsilon(X^{2/3+\epsilon})$. [Joint prime conditions and explicit moduli](joint_prime_counts.md)

At every weight, three rows whose two smallest parts each sum to at least five have coefficient gcd $\gg N^{1/3}$. If all three sums are at least six, the gcd exceeds $N^{1/2-\epsilon}$ for every fixed $\epsilon>0$ and sufficiently large $N$. [Pointwise common-divisor bounds](gcd_cutoffs.md)

For almost all weights $N$, every three rows share an individual prime exceeding $\exp(\sqrt{\log N}/\log\log N)$. More generally the number of weights in $[X,2X)$ where some triple of rows has no common prime greater than $y$ is $O(X\log^2(2y)/\log X)$.

The full assertion is proved here for every $N=5\cdot2^k+1$, for every weight with base-$p$ digit sum at most five for some prime $p\mid N-1$, and throughout growing intervals above powers of two.

The conjecture has been checked here for every $3\le N\le1{,}000{,}000{,}000$. Through 5,000 the table gives a smallest possible set of candidate common primes. The only weights through 5,000 requiring five primes are 699 and 717. For all sufficiently large $X$, at least $cX/(\log X)^2$ pairs of consecutive weights in $[X,2X]$ have minimum certificate size exactly four at both weights.

In any counterexample, select a cut (one part or the sum of two) from each of the two nonendpoint rows. When the ratio of the cuts is reduced, its smaller term $x$ must exceed both 4096 and $(3125N/108)^{1/5}$. If $x\le 10^6$, the stronger bound $159375N<108x^5$ holds.
