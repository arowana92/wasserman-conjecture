# Digit sums of cyclic shifts in the inverse cut

Let $p$ be a prime, let $D$ be a prime power coprime to $p$, and let $h\ge1$ satisfy $D\mid p^h-1$. Put $C=(p^h-1)/D$ and let $s_p$ denote base-$p$ digit sum. Suppose
$$
                \big(s_p(D)-1\big)s_p(C)<h(p-1). \tag{1}
$$
For every prime $q$ coprime to $pD$ and all $b,j\ge1$, the full conjecture holds at $1+Dp^b q^j$ if, for some $A=p^m\ge D$, either of the following conditions holds.

- $q^j\equiv-1\bmod A$.
- $q^j\equiv1\bmod A$ and $q^j\bmod D$ belongs to the group generated by $p$.

In particular, if $q\equiv-1\bmod A$ and $q^2\bmod D$ belongs to that group, **every pair of exponents** works. This includes the weights
$$
  1+73\cdot2^b\cdot131071^j,\qquad
  1+(2^{31}-1)2^b(2^{61}-1)^j,\qquad b,j\ge1.
$$

## Why a submask in the power orbit must be a single digit

At a failure the [one-numerator reduction](static_square_cofactors.md#at-most-two-possible-numerators) and [same cut at both varying primes](three_factor_exponent_rectangles.md#the-same-cut-in-both-bases) give $0<x<D$ such that
$$
      xp^b q^j\equiv1\bmod D,\quad \gcd(x,N)\ge3,\quad
      xq^j+(D-x)q^j\text{ adds without a carry in base }p.
$$
If $q^j\equiv-1\bmod A$, the two residues add to $2A-D\ge A$. If $q^j\equiv1\bmod A$, put $y=D-x$. The ordinary addition $x+y=D$ is carry-free, since $x,y<A$ and $D<A$ (equality is impossible by coprimality). Consequently $s_p(y)=s_p(D)-s_p(x)$.

In the second case $x\equiv p^a\bmod D$ for some $a\ge0$. Thus $Cx\equiv p^a C\bmod p^h-1$. Both $C$ and $Cx$ are strictly between zero and $p^h-1$, so the $h$ digits of $Cx$ are a cyclic shift of those of $C$. In particular $s_p(Cx)=s_p(C)$. Subtracting from the all-$(p-1)$ word $p^h-1$ gives
$$
 h(p-1)-s_p(C)=s_p(Cy)
       \le s_p(C)s_p(y)=s_p(C)\big(s_p(D)-s_p(x)\big).
$$
The inequality follows by expanding the products of digits before carrying. If $s_p(x)\ge2$, this contradicts (1). Hence $x$ is a power of $p$, impossible since $p\nmid N$.

One family satisfying (1) is every prime power of the form
$$
                    D=1+p^r+\cdots+p^{(n-1)r},\qquad n\ge2.
$$
Take $h=nr$ and $C=p^r-1$. Here $s_p(C)s_p(D)=h(p-1)$ exactly. The case $p=2,r=3,n=3$ gives $D=73$. The prime $q=2^{17}-1$ is $-1$ modulo 128, and $2^{18}\equiv1\bmod73$ gives $q\equiv-2^{-1}\bmod73$. The larger example has $D=2^{31}-1$, $q=2^{61}-1$ and $2^{62}\equiv1\bmod D$. In both examples $q^2\equiv2^{-2}\bmod D$.

## A cofactor bound uniform over exponent classes

Let $M\ge2$, $B=p^{M-v_p(2)}$, and take a prime $q\equiv p^M-1\bmod p^M B$. Let $\lambda_p=(2\sin(\pi/(4p+2)))^{-1}$, $\beta_p=\log_p\lambda_p$, and $K=p(p+1)/2$. For $2\le Y\le B$ the fraction of unit classes $e\bmod B$ which could fail to certify all weights
$$
  1+Dp^b q^j,\qquad b,j\ge1,\quad j/p^{v_p(j)}\equiv e\bmod B,\qquad
  D\le Y\text{ a prime power coprime to }p
$$
is at most
$$
                      O_p\big(Y^{\log_p K}\,B^{\beta_p-1}\big). \tag{2}
$$
The same bound holds simultaneously for the additional cofactor types in the [four-prime cut theorem](three_factor_exponent_rectangles.md#extension-to-four-predecessor-primes) when they are coprime to $pq$. In binary, for example, $Y=B^{0.19}$ gives a fraction tending to zero as $M$ grows.

Odd exponents already work by the negative-residue test. At an even exponent, write $v=v_p(j)$. The [power expansion](prime_bases_at_geometric_exponents.md#an-explicit-prime-progression-for-all-outer-exponents) gives
$$
                 q^j=1+p^{M+v}Z,\qquad Z\equiv-e\bmod B .
$$
Consequently a failure needs a cut $x,y>0$ with $x+y=D$ that adds without carrying at **both** scales $1$ and $Z$, in the low $\log_p B$ positions at the latter. The first requirement is exact since $D<p^{M+v}$, except possibly equality when $v=0$ and $Y=B=p^M$, where $p\nmid D$ still excludes equality.

For one cut the [digit-block estimate](three_factor_exponent_rectangles.md#how-many-exponent-terms-can-pass-a-cut) bounds the number of classes by $(p/\lambda_p)^{v_p(xy)}B^{\beta_p}$. It remains to sum the weights over the first, carry-free cuts. Put $m=\lceil\log_p(Y+1)\rceil$. There are $K$ possibilities at each of the $m$ positions of an ordered pair of digits whose sum is less than $p$. If $v_p(xy)=s\ge1$, exactly one entry is divisible by $p^s$, since $p\nmid D$. There are at most $2p^s K^{m-s}$ corresponding pairs. For $s=0$, use $K^m$. The geometric series over $s$ converges because $p^2/(K\lambda_p)<1$, using $\lambda_p\ge(p+1)/2$ and $4p<(p+1)^2$. Thus the total weight is $O_p(K^m)=O_p(Y^{\log_p K})$. Division by the number of unit classes proves (2).

## Counts at the prime $2^{61}-1$

For every prime $q\equiv2^{61}-1\bmod2^{121}$ and every odd prime power $D\le250$, the same odd classes modulo $2^{60}$ certify all positive binary exponents and all inner exponents having their odd part in the class. The guaranteed proportion exceeds **99.9937%**. At the particular prime $q=2^{61}-1$ it exceeds **99.9962%**. The denominators of both counts are $2^{59}$.

| Bound on $D$ | Unordered cuts for the progression | Upper bound on exceptional classes | Cuts for the particular prime | Upper bound on exceptional classes |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | 165 | 11,376,825,559,964 | 88 | 5,222,790,089,452 |
| 250 | 760 | 35,935,947,104,515 | 492 | 21,688,449,846,682 |

For the progression, retain only binary-disjoint cuts $x,D-x$ with $\gcd(x,D)=1$ and some odd prime divisor of $x$. At the particular prime, at least one such divisor $\ell$ must divide $N$. The simultaneous conditions $xW\equiv1\bmod D$, $DW\equiv-1\bmod\ell$ specify one residue modulo $D\ell$ for $W=2^b q^j$. Since $j$ is even, this residue must belong to the subgroup generated by 2 and $q^2$. After testing both orientations, count each surviving unordered cut once, using the two independent low-bit counts in the [preceding table](geometric_exponent_pairs.md#explicit-exponent-classes-for-a-fixed-prime). Summing the counts bounds their union.

At the particular prime the subgroup test leaves no cut at all for $D=3,5,7,9,17,31,49,97$ through 250. All exponents work at these cofactors. Run **python3 check_cyclic_cut_digits.py** to verify the tables, the two examples, and the digit-sum comparison.
