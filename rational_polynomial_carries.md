# Multinomial valuations on rational polynomial families of prime powers

Fix a prime $p$, a positive integer $D$ coprime to $p$, and finitely many lists of signed integer coefficients $c_{ij}$. Consider the parts and their total
$$
 A_i=D^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^d c_{ij}p^{e_j},\qquad
 N=\sum_i A_i,\qquad 0=e_0<e_1<\cdots<e_d .
$$
Include the coefficient list of $DN$ in the bounds below. At parameters where all parts are nonnegative integers, put $v=v_p(N!/\prod_i A_i!)$. Zero parts are allowed in the valuation formula. Wasserman's problem uses positive parts.

**After a computable threshold, increasing a single gap $e_{j+1}-e_j$ by $h=\operatorname{ord}_D(p)$ increases $v$ by a fixed nonnegative integer.** Take $h=1$ for $D=1$. More precisely, partition each gap into its exact value below the threshold and its residue modulo $h$ above the threshold. On each resulting cell, $v$ is affine in the numbers of whole periods inserted into the long gaps. Any inserted period preserves the other increments. In particular the carry-free parameters on a cell are determined by the value at its minimum gaps and the increments on its axes, when the parts there are nonnegative integers.

This also applies when the exponents are integer linear forms in several nonnegative parameters, for example after expanding a power of a sum of sparse base-$p$ terms. Partition into the finitely many weak orders of the forms, combining coefficients of tied forms and adjoining a zero event if necessary. On each cell, integrality, the gap conditions and the formula then give a finite description by linear inequalities and congruences of the nonnegative integral parameters having any prescribed valuation. Sign is constant on the cells in the sense of negative versus nonnegative. A zero part may become positive under insertion. The minimum-gap calculation can use abstract gap lengths even if the linear parameterization itself misses that corner.

**Bounded digit sum.** Fix a bound $b$ and rational polynomials $f_i$ with a common denominator prime to $p$. One can decide whether there is any positive integer $s$ with $s_p(s)\le b$ such that all $f_i(s)$ are positive integers and their multinomial valuation has a specified value. One can also decide whether infinitely many distinct such $s$ exist. Enumerate canonical digit multiplicities (each between 1 and $p-1$) of total at most $b$. Represent $s$ as the sum of those multiples of $p^{n_j}$ at strictly increasing positions. The polynomial expansions have finitely many monomials whose positions are nonnegative integer linear forms in the $n_j$. The preceding finite partition therefore reduces existence to integer linear arithmetic with congruences. For each exponent-order cell, nonnegativity follows from the common terminal state. Positivity of a nonnegative part is equivalent to its digit sum being at least one, another affine condition by the same division argument. Infinitude is equivalent to unboundedness of the largest digit position in at least one of these finitely many cases. This decision procedure need not be efficient. The computation at cofactor 35 instead uses small explicit prefix trees.

## Reading the division from the units place

Let $C$ be the maximum of the absolute coefficients after combining ties. For each numerator separately, keep a signed integer $u$ initialized to zero. At a column with coefficient $c$ (zero at an empty column), output the digit $z\in[0,p)$ determined by $Dz\equiv u+c\bmod p$ and replace $u$ by $(u+c-Dz)/p$. After $k$ columns, the partial numerator equals $D$ times the low output word plus $u p^k$. Hence these are the digits of the rational input modulo each $p^k$. The state always satisfies
$$|u|\le M=D+\lceil C/(p-1)\rceil,$$
by induction on the update.

Choose $K$ with $p^K>M+D$. After $K$ consecutive empty columns the state lies in $[-D,0]$, since it equals $(u_{\rm in}-Dz_*)/p^K$ with $0\le z_*<p^K$. The endpoints are fixed on empty columns. In the interior the map permutes the representatives of the nonzero residues modulo $D$ by multiplication by $p^{-1}$. Thus $h$ further empty columns return every state to itself. A gap of at least $K+1$ includes the required $K$ blanks after its coefficient column.

Run the same calculation on the total. Let $u_N,u_i$ be the states just past a column. The outgoing carry in adding the part digits, counting a carry of size two twice, is $(u_N-\sum_i u_i)/D$. This follows by subtracting the partial numerator identities, and is nonnegative because the low digit words of the parts sum to the low word of their total modulo $p^k$. Inserting a period in a long empty stretch preserves the states and digit words outside the insertion. Its contribution is the sum of the outgoing carries in the repeated word. Kummer's formula proves the assertion. Integrality is fixed on the cell since $p^h\equiv1\bmod D$. For an integral value the terminal division state divided by $D$ is the quotient by the power of $p$ just past the last column. This state is unchanged, fixing the sign criterion. [Bergman, Lemma 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.0607)

## A shorter threshold for a univariate polynomial

For $A_i=G_i(p^n)/D$, use any $m\ge1$ with $p^m>1+\max|[X^j]G_i|$, including the total polynomial. Assume positive leading coefficients, or an identically zero polynomial. For all $n\ge m$, in each residue class modulo $h$ it suffices to evaluate $v(n)$ and $v(n+h)$ at the first two exponents. Their difference is the increment for the entire class wherever the parts are integral.

To see the shorter threshold, normalize the signed coefficients of each numerator in base $p^n$ by borrowing from the next column. An incoming borrow is zero or minus one. The adjusted coefficient $b$ has $|b|<p^m$. Below the leading column the block digit is $b$ if $b\ge0$, or $p^n+b$ otherwise. Its low $m$ digits are fixed, and its remaining $n-m$ digits are all zero or all $p-1$. The leading column can also be padded with zeros. Now divide the numerator by $D$ from its most significant base-$p$ digit. A constant run of $h$ zeros sends a remainder $r$ to $p^h r\bmod D$, and a run of $h$ digits $p-1$ sends it to $p^h(r+1)-1\bmod D$. Both restore $r$. The quotient digit sum therefore gains a constant per period. Apply Legendre's digit-sum formula to the parts and total. The valuation increment cannot be negative, since an infinite arithmetic progression of nonnegative valuations would then eventually turn negative.

For integer coefficients, the corresponding digit-sum identities for adding or subtracting a separated block appear in Proposition 2.1 of Hare, Laishram and Stoll. [Stolarsky’s conjecture and the sum of digits of polynomial values](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.4169)

The [fourth-power application](fourth_power_cofactor_35.md) uses $p=3,D=16$. It determines every surviving offset $|d|\le10000$ for all positive exponents in $2s=3^n+d$. The computation includes negative polynomial coefficients. The checker also tests two independently varied gaps with signed coefficient lists and several denominators, computing each valuation by factorial quotients.
